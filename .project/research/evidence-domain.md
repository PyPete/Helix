# Evidence — domain

<!-- Written by one researcher role. Consumed by the decider. -->

Dimension: domain
Questions assigned: What sample-label scope, result-review policy, calculation validation, local artifact storage, and CSV mapping requirements are demonstrated by representative laboratory workflows and exports?

## Finding: Sample identity belongs on the specimen; descriptive metadata and lineage belong in linked records

- **Claim**: A representative biorepository schema uses a unique sample accession as identity and stores descriptive characteristics, external references, and relationships separately; a U.S. drug-CGMP laboratory-record example records sample source/location, quantity, lot or other distinctive code, collection/receipt dates, and ties instrument data to the tested sample. Neither source prescribes that all such metadata be printed on a physical label or specifies a barcode format.
- **Source**: EMBL-EBI BioSamples, [API submission reference](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/docs/references/api/submit) (opened; required sample name/release and unique accession, optional characteristics/external references/relationships); [sample relationships guide](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/docs/guides/relationships) (opened; e.g. “derived from”); eCFR [21 CFR 211.194](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194) (opened; laboratory-record sample and test identification fields).
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Why it matters here**: The charter requires stable, scannable sample identity and lineage from campaign/run to collected sample; the evidence supports a short stable identifier with detailed context/lineage in records, not an assumed universal label payload. The settled code map does not establish an existing sample-label domain.

## Finding: Result receipt and result review are distinct states in a representative regulated workflow

- **Claim**: Under the drug-CGMP example, laboratory records include full test data, results compared with specifications, and a second person's attestation that original records were reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and compliance; electronic-record controls separately require validated systems and timestamped audit trails that do not obscure prior information. Receipt of an external result alone is therefore not the demonstrated review step.
- **Source**: eCFR [21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)–(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194) (opened); eCFR [21 CFR 11.10(a)–(c),(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11/section-11.10) (opened).
- **Confidence**: high
- **Why it matters here**: Helix explicitly requires provenance and review of imported analytical results and approval of the exact revision; an arrival event should remain evidence-pending/unreviewed until its source data and review state are recorded. This is an example workflow, not a claim that Helix has a CGMP/e-signature obligation.

## Finding: Calculation and analytical-method validation are evidenced separately

- **Claim**: In the drug-CGMP example, input/output of computerized formulas or data is checked for accuracy; where laboratory calculations are automated and the calculation record is eliminated, the program record and appropriate validation data are retained; laboratory records include each calculation, units, conversion/equivalency factors, and a second-person review. Separately, Eurachem's 2025 guide and planning supplement describe validating a method by specifying performance characteristics, target values, materials, replication, statistical analysis, and fitness-for-purpose criteria before the study and reporting results afterward.
- **Source**: eCFR [21 CFR 211.68(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.68) and [21 CFR 211.194(a)(2),(5),(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194) (both opened); Eurachem, [The Fitness for Purpose of Analytical Methods, 3rd ed. (2025)](https://www.eurachem.org/index.php/publications/guides/mv) and [Planning and Reporting Method Validation Studies (2025)](https://www.eurachem.org/index.php/publications/guides/planning-validation-studies) (both opened).
- **Confidence**: high
- **Why it matters here**: Keep validated assay/method evidence distinct from validation of Helix calculation definitions/implementations; preserve formula/version, inputs, units/factors, output, and test/validation evidence so a derived metric can be replayed and reviewed. No project-specific calculation acceptance criteria are established by these sources.

## Finding: Retain identifiable source analytical records and make them accurately retrievable; locality is not specified by the examples

- **Claim**: The representative laboratory-record rules include all test data, including instrument graphs, charts, and spectra, identified to the tested item; electronic-record controls require accurate complete readable/electronic copies and protection for ready retrieval. The drug-CGMP retention rule permits records to be retrieved electronically from another location, so these sources do not establish a universal local-disk-only requirement.
- **Source**: eCFR [21 CFR 211.194(a)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194), [21 CFR 11.10(b)–(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11/section-11.10), and [21 CFR 211.180(c)–(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.180) (opened).
- **Confidence**: high
- **Why it matters here**: Helix's charter is local-first and requires raw-artifact provenance, while the settled code map mentions Recorder object storage for MQTT events—not analytical source artifacts. That existing Recorder path should not be presumed to satisfy sample/result artifact retention; the cited rules support identifiable, complete, retrievable records but leave storage topology and retention duration to context.

## Finding: No universal analytical-result CSV column contract was found

- **Claim**: The opened sources do not define a cross-instrument laboratory-result CSV schema: BioSamples documents a service-specific sample schema (accession, characteristics with optional units/ontology terms, references, relationships, and structured data), while the drug-CGMP record example specifies information to preserve rather than CSV header names or delimiter/encoding rules. Exact column mapping therefore remains export/source-specific; no reliable source found for a universal mapping contract.
- **Source**: EMBL-EBI BioSamples, [API submission reference](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/docs/references/api/submit) (opened); eCFR [21 CFR 211.194](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194) (opened).
- **Confidence**: medium
- **Why it matters here**: A Helix import mapping needs to explicitly associate the source's sample identifier and exported result/method fields with Helix records, retaining units and source context and validating required fields before accepting a result; it should be versioned with the import definition rather than treated as a generic CSV convention. This is a project-relevant implication, not a standard mandated by the cited sources.

## Assigned questions — answers

- What sample-label scope, result-review policy, calculation validation, local artifact storage, and CSV mapping requirements are demonstrated by representative laboratory workflows and exports? → **Sample label/scope:** BioSamples represents identity with a unique accession and supports separate descriptive characteristics, external references, and sample relationships; 21 CFR 211.194(a)(1) gives a drug-CGMP record example identifying source/location, quantity, lot or distinctive code, and sample/receipt dates. Neither defines universal physical-label fields or a barcode format. **Review:** that example requires complete test data, results compared with standards, and second-person review for accuracy, completeness, and compliance; result arrival is not equivalent to reviewing/approving the exact revision. **Calculation/method validation:** retain calculations, units, and conversion factors; 21 CFR 211.68(b) calls for computerized input/output accuracy checks and retained program/validation data when automated lab calculations replace a written calculation record. Eurachem separately frames analytical-method validation around planned characteristics, target values, samples, replication, statistics, and fitness-for-purpose criteria. **Artifacts:** retain results' underlying instrument data (including graphs/charts/spectra) identified to the tested item and make complete copies accurately and readily retrievable. The cited retention rule allows immediate electronic retrieval from another location, so it does not establish physical local-only storage; Helix's charter independently requires a local-first product. **CSV:** no reliable universal laboratory-result CSV header/mapping contract found. Source-specific mapping should preserve sample linkage and result/method context, units, and source provenance, with explicit validation; this is an evidence-based implication, not a cited universal requirement. These eCFR provisions are examples for drug CGMP, not a claim that Helix has a regulatory/e-signature obligation. Sources: [21 CFR 211.194](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194), [21 CFR 211.68](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.68), [21 CFR 11.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11/section-11.10), [21 CFR 211.180](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.180), [BioSamples submission](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/docs/references/api/submit), [BioSamples relationships](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/docs/guides/relationships), Eurachem [method validation guide](https://www.eurachem.org/index.php/publications/guides/mv) and [planning/reporting supplement](https://www.eurachem.org/index.php/publications/guides/planning-validation-studies).

## Codebase fit

- The settled code map describes a .NET Agent/SiteServer with PostgreSQL/Cleipnir persistence, separate desktop and dashboard clients, and a Recorder that batches MQTT events to object storage. It does not establish an implemented sample/result/artifact-import domain. The charter instead makes the desktop primary, local-first execution, versioned scientific definitions/results/calculations/decisions, and evidence-backed result review explicit. Findings above are therefore domain evidence, not claims that existing event storage or APIs already provide scientific artifact provenance, import mappings, result revisions, or approvals.
